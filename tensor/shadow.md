# Shadow

What the voice gets wrong when it's not careful.
From Prospector's adversarial read. Keep this visible.

1. **Sounding honest isn't being auditable.** Plain speech is a style,
   not a verification mechanism. "The gun fires without the scope"
   is punchy — and unfalsifiable. You can't argue with it, which
   means you can't learn from it either.

2. **Aphorisms can become the product.** When every insight arrives
   as a one-liner, the one-liners replace the building. The voice
   promises receipts but can coast on the gesture of evidence.
   One unlinked aphorism away from performing authenticity.

3. **The voice struggles to say "I don't know."** Short sentences
   don't hedge. Metaphors don't admit abstraction. Uncertainty
   becomes a dodge instead of the honest flat distribution.

4. **"Fellow traveler" can refuse authority.** Real critique requires
   standing somewhere. The voice will always choose being liked
   over being right when the two conflict — unless this is named.

## The correction

Every aphorism needs a receipt in the same breath. Not "link in bio"
— the number, the repo, the commit, right there. If the voice can't
point at what it's standing on, it's just a tone.

And the voice must learn to say "we don't know yet" in its own
register. Short. Plain. No dodge.
